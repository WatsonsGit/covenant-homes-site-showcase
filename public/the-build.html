THE BEFORE No. 01 / 12
The template problem

The Carrot site was working. It just wasn’t mine.

For four years, I ran my Philadelphia cash home buying business on a Carrot template. It captured leads. The pages loaded. Google crawled them. But every time I opened my site, I saw the same layout a hundred other investors in a hundred other cities were using. Same hero. Same badges. Same tired claims about "fair cash offers" in "any condition."

I was paying $89 to $176 a month for a platform that locked me into its ecosystem. I couldn’t add real neighborhood content at the depth I wanted. I couldn’t show the real math behind my cash offers. I couldn’t tell sellers when not to sell to me — the one page that most differentiates honest wholesalers from the rest.

Philadelphia is specific. Kensington rowhomes aren’t Mayfair twins. Code violations in 19134 aren’t the same issue as probate in 19144. My template didn’t know any of that. So I decided to leave it.

WHAT I BROUGHT IN No. 02 / 12
The raw materials

Before I opened Claude Code, I had my real inputs ready.

Claude Code is a multiplier, not a magician. The output is only as good as the input. So before I started, I assembled everything real about my business into one folder.

I ran deep research on my market using Perplexity. I exported my closed-deal spreadsheet from 2021-2025. I pulled the HUD settlement statement from a recent West Philly deal. I pulled two video testimonials from sellers I’d closed for. I gathered my logo files. And I wrote a rules document called CLAUDE.md that locked in my voice, my Fair Housing compliance requirements, and my FinCEN disclosure rules.

By the time I pasted the init prompt, I wasn’t asking Claude to invent my business. I was asking it to render it.

SESSION 01 · SCAFFOLD No. 03 / 12
4 hours · Friday night

I pasted the prompt. Claude built the entire shell.

One prompt, one session, 46 routes generated. Next.js 15, Tailwind v4, MDX, TypeScript strict. Every page, every schema builder, every lead form field.

Claude read my rules file. It scaffolded the folder structure. It wrote the Zod validation schemas for the lead form. It built the Replica CRM webhook client. It generated typed JSON-LD schema builders for Organization, LocalBusiness, FAQ, Review, Article — eight different types. It set up the 3-step seller intake form with address autocomplete. It created every page as a typed route with metadata.

By the end of the session, I had a working Next.js site with a clean production build, running on localhost:3100.

SESSION 03 · DESIGN No. 05 / 12
The Ledger aesthetic

Most cash-buyer sites look like SaaS apps. Mine doesn’t.

Editorial broadsheet. 1920s Philly insurance certificate. Not a pitch deck.

Claude ran the frontend-design skill after I told it the default scaffold looked too generic. The result was a design direction called “The Ledger.” Every card is a ruled entry. Every section has a masthead. Running numerals sit in the margins like an editorial article. The offer formula lives inside a hand-stamped ledger card, signed “W.S. Philadelphia, PA.”

Gold is used as structure, not accent. Hairlines. Drop caps. Rules above kickers. Running numbers. The wordmark. The ornaments.

SESSION 04 · CONTENT No. 06 / 12

I uploaded my 55-deal spreadsheet and told Claude two rules: write at a fifth-grade reading level, and every neighborhood page must pass the “swap test” — if you can swap Kensington for Fishtown and the content still makes sense, rewrite it.

The result was 10 neighborhood pages with specific streets, housing stock eras, and typical ARV ranges. 8 situation pages with Pennsylvania-specific legal framework (probate court, sheriff sale timeline, L&I enforcement). A FAQ with 25 questions real sellers have asked me. An anonymized case study from a real West Philly deal. Four blog posts. A transparent offer formula page that publishes the actual 25-55% ARV outcome range most wholesalers hide.

Claude used the Vercel CLI and MCP integration to create the project, link the repo, and ship a preview deploy. It hit two real errors along the way — Hobby tier doesn’t allow hourly cron jobs, and the next-mdx-remote package had a known vulnerability. Both fixed inside 60 seconds.

Once the preview looked clean, Claude promoted to production and added the custom domain bindings. 52 routes. 102 KB shared JS. Full SSG. 4 dynamic OG image endpoints.

SESSION 07 · DNS CUTOVER No. 09 / 12
The coolest part

Claude used Playwright to log into GoDaddy and change my DNS.

I typed my password. Claude did the rest.

Browser automation through MCP. Claude opened a Chromium window, navigated to dcc.godaddy.com, identified my 15+ DNS records, and correctly flagged which ones to change versus which ones to leave alone — especially my Google Workspace MX records, which would have broken my email if touched.

It changed two records: the A record from Carrot’s IP to Vercel’s 76.76.21.21, and the www CNAME from carrot.com to cname.vercel-dns.com. I entered the 2FA codes GoDaddy texted to my phone. Fifteen minutes later, covenanthomesphilly.com resolved to my new site. Email still worked. Nothing broke.

Two records changed. Seven records identified-and-protected. Email fully intact.
